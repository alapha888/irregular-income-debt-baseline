The three-tier rule this calculator follows

Tier 1 — buffer under 1 month of essentials: pay minimums only. Every spare dollar goes to the buffer first. A low month with no buffer is how people re-borrow to pay debt.
Tier 2 — buffer at 1 month: minimums + buffer top-up if needed, then all extra to one target debt (snowball or avalanche). High months pay down debt; low months fall back to minimums without touching the buffer.
Tier 3 — buffer at 3 months: throw everything above essentials + minimums at the target debt. The buffer is what lets you be aggressive without risking a missed payment.

Why the baseline month matters: a budget built on average income breaks in a low month. This calculator sizes everything to your lowest month, then treats a good month as a bonus to allocate — never as the plan.

What this does not do

  • It is a baseline planner, not financial advice. It does not know your loan terms, fees, penalty rates, or tax situation.
  • The debt-free estimate assumes every future month equals your lowest month — deliberately conservative. Real months with higher income will finish sooner.
  • It assumes fixed APRs and no new borrowing. Variable rates and new charges change the numbers.
